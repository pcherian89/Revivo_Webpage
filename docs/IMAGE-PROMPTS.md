# Revivo website — image plan and ChatGPT prompt pack

Images for revivosportech.com, generated in ChatGPT and placed into the site by Claude.

**Chosen direction: Night editorial illustration.** Painted, clearly illustrated scenes of real sporting life at
night or dusk: warm, human and varied, but dark, calm and premium so they sit inside Revivo's black, lime-signal
design. This borrows what worked in the Game Keralam prototype (people, many sports, a picture for every module,
an obviously illustrative style) without its bright, busy, brochure look.

Rules that always apply (from `CLAUDE.md`): no fake software screens or dashboards; no invented clients, logos,
crests, kits or real venues; no real or famous people and no founder likeness; nothing medical or ECG-like; no
robots, brains, holograms or blue/purple "AI" light. Images are illustrative mood pictures, never "our work".

never exact. The site draws Revivo's own thin lime signal into and across the images, so every picture connects to
the brand in exactly the same way.

## 2. How to make them (step by step)

1. Open a **new chat** in ChatGPT.
2. Paste the **Master style prompt** (section 3) and send it.
3. Make the **hero (#1)** first. Ask for 3 versions and pick the best. This becomes the style anchor.
4. Say: **"Use this image as the style and palette reference for every image that follows."**
5. Make the rest **in the same chat**, one at a time, in batches of four (section 4). Start each request with
   "Match the master style and the approved reference." and end it with the **negative line** (section 3).
6. If an image drifts, reply with one of the fixes in section 5. Don't rewrite the whole prompt.
7. Check every image against the **reject list** (section 6).
8. Download each at full size and name it as in section 7. If you need a new chat later, upload the approved hero
   and two area images first as references.
9. Upload them to Claude. Claude grades them as one set, crops and compresses them, places them, and sends you
   laptop and phone screenshots before anything goes live.

## 3. Master style prompt (paste this first)

> For this whole chat you are making one consistent series of illustrations for Revivo, a global company that
> designs custom digital systems for sports organisations.
>
> MEDIUM: premium editorial illustration in a matte, painterly gouache style. It must clearly read as an
> illustration, never a photo: confident visible brushwork, simplified shapes, soft painted edges, limited palette.
>
> LIGHT AND COLOUR: night or dusk. One warm practical light source per scene (a floodlight, a doorway, a desk lamp,
> a tunnel light) with rim light on figures, gentle haze and deep shadows that fall to near-black (#0A0A0B) and
> deep graphite. Low saturation everywhere; only warm amber and off-white (#F5F5F2) highlights keep colour.
>
> COMPOSITION: calm and uncluttered. Usually 1–4 people, never a crowd collage. One continuous believable place
> with a clear foreground, middle and background. At least 40% of every frame is quiet dark space.
>
> PEOPLE: warm and human, absorbed in what they are doing, mostly three-quarter or profile views. Faces are
> stylised and painted with simple features, never photoreal, never close-up portraits, never a real or famous
> person. Mix ages, genders, skin tones, body types and abilities (include para-sport and wheelchair users). Show
> many roles, not only athletes: organisers, venue crew, volunteers, administrators, fans, families, coaches,
> officials, analysts, community players.
>
> SETTING: global and unspecific: no landmarks, no single country, no palm-tree postcard scenery. Vary the sports
> across the series. Plain kit in muted solid colours.
>
> Confirm, then wait for my first request.

**Negative line (end every request with this):**
> No text, letters, numbers or signage; no logos, crests, flags, jersey numbers or real kits; no screens showing an
> interface, chart or dashboard (screens turned away or dark); no lime, neon, yellow-green, blue or purple glow;
> no robots, brains, holograms, particles, glass shapes or ECG lines; nothing medical; no celebrities or trophies;
> no over-saturated colour or glossy plastic skin.

## 4. The prompts

Start every request with **"Match the master style and the approved reference."** and end it with the
**negative line**.

### #1 Homepage hero (wide 16:9)
> A wide night scene of one floodlit community sports precinct where several venues share one painted landscape.
> Keep it calm: only three or four activities, small in the frame — a youth football session with a coach, a
> wheelchair basketball pair on an outdoor court, two runners on a track bend, a volunteer carrying cones. The
> focal point is the softly glowing centre circle of the football pitch, placed in the right third of the frame at
> about 40% of the height. All the people and light sit in the right half; the left half is dark haze and night
> sky fading to near-black, empty for a headline. A long low perimeter wall runs perfectly straight across the
> whole image at one-third of its height, its top edge catching faint warm-white light (do NOT colour it lime or
> make it glow).

### #2–5 The four areas (3:2; interest in the middle band, ends darker)

**#2 Events & Operations**
> The night before an outdoor sports festival: crew raising a lighting gantry, a volunteer laying out lane cones,
> an organiser with a clipboard pointing across the site. Several sports areas being prepared side by side under
> work lights.

**#3 Growth & Engagement**
> Fans and families arriving at a floodlit ground at dusk: a child on a parent's shoulders, two friends in plain
> muted scarves, someone filming the warm-up on a phone held away from us, the glow of the ground beyond the gate.

**#4 Athletes & Performance**
> Evening training seen through the open doors of a sports centre: a sprinter settling into the blocks outside,
> a boxer working pads with a coach inside, a young netball squad gathered around their coach. Focused, warm mood.

**#5 Management & Reporting**
> A clubhouse at night with its upper windows lit: inside, a small committee around a table with paper folders and
> mugs, one person gesturing towards the window. Below, the community pitches they run are in use under lights.

### #6–21 The sixteen capabilities (3:2; keep the bottom third dark and quiet)

**Events & Operations**

**#6 Event Planning & Coordination**
> At dawn on a half-built event site, an organiser and a volunteer lead lean over a folding table with a clipboard
> of blank sheets and a two-way radio, agreeing the plan, while crew raise a gantry behind them.

**#7 Venue & Facility Management**
> An indoor multi-sport hall after hours with one bank of lights on over the far court. A facilities manager with a
> torch checks a basketball hoop from a ladder while a colleague wheels an equipment cart along the court lines.

**#8 Registration & Administration**
> A registration desk at a junior cricket academy in the early evening: an administrator hands a blank form to a
> parent while a child in plain whites waits, neat stacks of blank paper and a rubber stamp on the desk.

**#9 Staff & Volunteer Management**
> Before sunrise at a city road race, in a staging tent lit by warm lamps: a volunteer coordinator briefs a mixed
> group in plain unbranded hi-vis vests — a student, a retiree, a wheelchair user, a parent with a flask — while
> others take vests from a rail.

**Growth & Engagement**

**#10 Social Media & Campaign Management**
> At the sideline of a youth football match at dusk, a young media volunteer films the play on a phone held away
> from us while a club staff member beside her reviews shots on a closed notebook. Players beyond are backlit.

**#11 Sponsorship Management**
> Pitchside at dusk on a rugby ground: a partnerships manager walks two visitors in plain business clothes along
> the touchline, gesturing to a row of completely blank, unlit boards, while a ground crew member fixes one in
> place. Players warm up behind. The boards have no graphics.

**#12 Fan Engagement & Ticketing**
> Fans moving through floodlit stadium turnstiles at night: a grandmother with a grandchild, two teenagers, a man
> in a plain scarf, with a steward gently waving them through. Warm, welcoming, no signage.

**#13 Membership & Customer Management**
> Early morning at the welcome desk of a community fitness club: a staff member greets a new member while another
> member walks in with a towel over the shoulder, the training floor softly lit beyond.

**Athletes & Performance**

**#14 Athlete Management & Development**
> A coach and a teenage footballer sit on a locker-room bench under a single light, talking through a paper
> notebook; beside them, a row of boots in ascending sizes from child to adult suggests years of growth.

**#15 Training & Performance Monitoring**
> A dewy training ground at first light: a coach with a stopwatch watches a runner mid-stride between neat rows of
> small cones, breath visible in the cold air.

**#16 Video Analysis & Scouting**

**#17 Athlete Career & Welfare**

**Management & Reporting**

## 5. Quick fixes when an image drifts

| Problem | Say |
|---|---|
| Too bright or colourful | "Darker night, 60% less saturation, only the warm highlights keep colour, more black space." |
| Too busy | "Fewer people (1–4), one continuous place, clear foreground and background, left side empty." |
| Plastic or waxy faces | "Painted, simplified faces with visible brushstrokes, people absorbed in their task, three-quarter or profile views." |
| Gibberish text on shirts or boards | "Plain solid-colour shirts, no numbers, blank boards." (or edit the area: "make this shirt plain") |
| Neon or lime line appears | "Remove the glowing line; no lime or neon anywhere." |
| Looks like one country | "Global, unspecific city and climate, mixed people." |
| A screen shows a dashboard | "Screen turned away or dark; blank whiteboard." |

---

## 6. Reject an image if…

- hands, rackets, bats, balls, nets or wheelchairs are warped, or fingers are extra or merged
- **any** text, numbers, letters, signage or jersey numbers appear, even garbled
- logos, crests, sponsor marks, flags, team colours or a recognisable stadium appear
- faces are photoreal, close-up portraits, or look like a real person (or the founder)
- a screen shows anything (interface, chart, dashboard)
- there is lime/yellow-green glow, neon, or blue/purple light
- anything medical or ECG-like appears
- it looks like a bright brochure, a collage, or a crowd scene
- the dark space asked for (bottom third, left half) is filled

## 8. How Claude will place them

- One colour grade over the whole set so every picture matches, then cropped, compressed (AVIF/WebP) and loaded
  only when needed, so the site stays fast. The hero is kept light enough not to slow the first screen.
- Every image melts into the black background; text never sits on busy detail.
- Revivo's lime signal is drawn by the site: the hero line runs into the illustration and ends in the signal circle
  over its focal point; a thin lime line crosses each panel header; the area backdrops sit along the explorer's
  branches.
- Images stay still (no zooming or sliding); the running light remains the only motion. Reduced-motion users see
  everything static.
- Laptop and phone screenshots are sent for approval before anything goes live.

## Appendix: the alternative (not chosen) — dark documentary photography

Realistic night photography with no faces (only hands, backs and silhouettes). It is premium when it works, but
it feels colder without visible people, drifts more across 25+ images, shows AI flaws (hands, crowds, signage)
more, and a realistic "sponsor meeting" can look like real client work. If you ever prefer it, ask Claude for the
photography version of this pack.
